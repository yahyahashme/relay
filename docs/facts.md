## Source quality

- **Official:** World Bank; Banco Central de Reserva (BCR, the central bank and financial regulator); Superintendencia del Sistema Financiero (SSF, the financial supervisor); the Legislative Assembly (law texts); Dun & Bradstreet.
- **Established media:** El Diario de Hoy (elsalvador.com and eldiariodehoy.com), Diario El Mundo, Revista E&N (Estrategia & Negocios).
- **Research groups:** FUSAI's Observatorio MYPE with ASOMI (the microfinance association); CIOPS, the polling centre of Universidad Tecnológica de El Salvador (UTEC); Global Initiative Against Transnational Organized Crime (GI-TOC).
- **Weaker:** Diario El Salvador (government-aligned, as #50 notes). Used only where no one else has the number. The El Salvador in English links from context §19 are replaced below.

## 1. The market: what distributors sell to tiendas
- **Claim:** Distributors sell about $2 billion a year of stock to El Salvador's neighbourhood shops.
- **Number:** about US$2 billion a year (said Feb 2026)
- **Source:** Diario El Salvador, "La seguridad impulsó el crecimiento de las tiendas de barrio en un 23 %", 27 Feb 2026, https://diarioelsalvador.com/dedinero/la-seguridad-impulso-el-crecimiento-de-las-tiendas-de-barrio-en-un-23/763705
- **Exact quote:** "más los $2,000 millones que les venden las distribuidoras"
- **Translation:** "plus the $2,000 million that distributors sell them"
- **Use in:** one-pager / pitch / trifold / deck
- **Caveat:** Spoken by Conamype's president, Paul Steiner, who credits unnamed independent market studies. The article does not present it as a Distribución Dinámica finding. Cite it as "Conamype estimate, via Diario El Salvador (27 Feb 2026)". It comes from one official in a government-aligned paper, with no methods. Other outlets only repeat it.
- **Checked:** Claude, 9 Nov 2026 (link opened, quote matches the page)

## 2. How many shops
- **Claim:** El Salvador has 85,265 neighbourhood shops and small retailers (2025).
- **Number:** 85,265 (2025)
- **Source:** Diario El Mundo, "El Salvador supera las 85,000 tiendas de barrio tras crecer 23 % en una década", 24 Feb 2026, https://diario.elmundo.sv/economia/el-salvador-supera-las-85000-tiendas-de-barrio-tras-crecer-23-en-una-decada
- **Exact quote:** "en 2025 había a nivel nacional 85,265 tiendas de colonias o ventas detallistas"
- **Translation:** "in 2025 there were 85,265 neighbourhood shops or small retailers nationwide"
- **Use in:** one-pager / pitch / trifold / deck
- **Caveat:** Counted by Distribución Dinámica, a private distribution consultancy, and presented with Conamype. Methods not published. The total includes eateries, street vendors, tortilla shops, bookshops and smoothie stands, so don't call all 85,265 "neighbourhood shops" (see entry 3). Diario El Salvador reports the same total.
- **Checked:** Claude, 9 Nov 2026 (link opened, quote matches the page)

## 3. How many are neighbourhood shops proper
- **Claim:** About 50,000 of those businesses are neighbourhood shops; about 35,000 are other small retailers.
- **Number:** 50,133 neighbourhood shops + 35,142 other retailers (2025)
- **Source:** Diario El Mundo, "En El Salvador hay más de 300 pupuserías que también funcionan como tiendas", 1 Mar 2026, https://diario.elmundo.sv/economia/en-el-salvador-hay-mas-de-300-pupuserias-que-tambien-funcionan-como-tiendas
- **Exact quote:** "35,142 corresponden a otros detallistas y 50,133 a tiendas de colonia o barrio"
- **Translation:** "35,142 are other retailers and 50,133 are neighbourhood shops"
- **Use in:** pitch Q&A / deck (backup)
- **Caveat:**
  - Sources disagree: Diario El Salvador (entry 1's article) says other retailers are 18% of the total (about 15,000), while this split makes them 41%.
  - The split adds to 85,275, ten more than the total, probably a typo.
  - Safe wording: "85,265 shops and small retailers" or "more than 50,000 neighbourhood shops".
- **Checked:** Claude, 9 Nov 2026 (link opened, quote matches the page)

## 4. Growth in a decade
- **Claim:** Neighbourhood shops and small retailers grew 23% in ten years.
- **Number:** +23.3% (69,111 in 2015 to 85,265 in 2025)
- **Source:** Diario El Mundo, 24 Feb 2026 (same article as entry 2), https://diario.elmundo.sv/economia/el-salvador-supera-las-85000-tiendas-de-barrio-tras-crecer-23-en-una-decada
- **Exact quote:** "Las tiendas de colonias o ventas detallistas crecieron un 23.3 % en la última década, un impulso que se sostuvo desde 2022 debido a la reducción de la violencia, reveló un estudio presentado este martes por Distribución Dinámica."
- **Translation:** "Neighbourhood shops and small retailers grew 23.3% in the last decade, a boost sustained since 2022 by the drop in violence, according to a study presented this Tuesday by Distribución Dinámica."
- **Use in:** one-pager / deck
- **Caveat:**
  - Same mixed count as entry 2.
  - Growth was uneven: +58% in Greater San Salvador, +1% in the east.
  - The study says shop numbers fell from 2011 to 2020 because of gang extortion payments and then COVID.
  - Officials credit the security crackdown since 2022. State the number, not the politics.
- **Checked:** Claude, 10 Nov 2026 (link opened in a browser, quote copied from the page)

## 5. What tiendas sell, and why we never say $5B
- **Claim:** Neighbourhood shops sell about $3 billion a year to their customers.
- **Number:** about US$3 billion a year (said Feb 2026)
- **Source:** Diario El Salvador, 27 Feb 2026 (same article and speaker as entry 1), https://diarioelsalvador.com/dedinero/la-seguridad-impulso-el-crecimiento-de-las-tiendas-de-barrio-en-un-23/763705
- **Exact quote:** "Estudios de mercado independientes nos dicen que las tiendas de barrio venden aproximadamente $3,000 millones al año, más los $2,000 millones que les venden las distribuidoras. Estamos hablando de un canal que vale $5,000 millones anuales"
- **Translation:** "Independent market studies tell us that neighbourhood shops sell about $3,000 million a year, plus the $2,000 million that distributors sell them. We're talking about a channel worth $5,000 million a year."

- **Caveat:** The "$5 billion" headline adds what distributors sell to shops ($2B) and what shops sell to customers ($3B), which counts the same goods twice. Never use $5B as our market. Same attribution caveat as entry 1.
- **Checked:** Claude, 10 Nov 2026 (link opened in a browser, quote copied from the page)

## 7. Extortion used to close shops and delivery routes
- **Claim:** Before 2022, gang extortion closed shops and forced distributors to shut delivery routes.
- **Number:** extortion payments of about 1.7% of El Salvador's GDP (2015 estimate)
- **Source:**
  - GI-TOC, "Arrested development in northern Central America", 9 Feb 2022, https://globalinitiative.net/analysis/development-extortion-central-america/
  - Diario El Salvador, 27 Feb 2026 (entry 1's article), quoting Distribución Dinámica's director, Roberto Santiváñez
- **Exact quote:**
  - GI-TOC: "the public transport and delivery industries are among the worst affected"
  - Diario El Salvador: "La extorsión venía generando cierres de tiendas, cierres de las rutas de las diferentes distribuidoras; en lugar de abrir rutas, cerraban rutas, y así cada año iban reduciéndose los detallistas"
- **Translation:** GI-TOC is in English. Santiváñez: "Extortion was closing shops and closing the different distributors' routes; instead of opening routes, they were closing them, and so every year there were fewer retailers."
- **Use in:** pitch / deck (background only)
- **Caveat:** GI-TOC's sentence covers northern Central America, not El Salvador alone; the 1.7% is El Salvador's own figure. This is history: the government reports extortion fell sharply after the 2022 state of exception. Use it as background, not as a claim about today.
- **Checked:** Claude, 9 Nov 2026 (GI-TOC quote matches the page); 10 Nov 2026 (Santiváñez quote copied from the page)

## 9. Loan sharks
- **Claim:** Six in ten informal small businesses have borrowed from loan sharks. Most get the money the same day, at about 1,869% a year.
- **Number:** 62.2% have borrowed informally at some point; 68% get the money the same day; average rate about 1,869% a year (study presented Jul 2026)
- **Source:** El Diario de Hoy, "Seis de cada diez microempresas informales dependen de los préstamos con usureros", 24 Jul 2026, https://www.eldiariodehoy.com/negocios/microempresas-el-salvador-dependen-prestamos-usureros/86923/2026/
- **Exact quote:** "un 62.2% de estos pequeños negocios ha recurrido alguna vez a prestamistas informales"
- **Translation:** "62.2% of these small businesses have at some point turned to informal lenders"
- **Use in:** one-pager / pitch / trifold / deck
- **Caveat:**
  - Study by FUSAI's Observatorio MYPE and ASOMI.
  - "These small businesses" means micro and small businesses that run informally or semi-informally, not all of them.
  - The article gives no sample size or survey dates.
  - The 1,869% equals the rate CIOPS (UTEC) measured in March 2022, so it may be older data.
  - Other surveys give higher shares (CIOPS 2022: 80.9%).
  - Reasons for borrowing: 43.5% a family emergency, 41.7% to keep the business running.
  - 68%: "el 68% de quienes buscan dinero con un usurero lo recibe ese mismo día" ("68% of those who go to a loan shark get the money that same day").
  - 1,869%: "la tasa promedio que cobran los usureros ronda el 1,869% anual" ("the average rate loan sharks charge is around 1,869% a year"). Both copied from the page on 10 Nov 2026.
- **Checked:** Claude, 9 Nov 2026 (link opened, quote matches the page)

## 10. What loan sharks cost
- **Claim:** A loan shark charges about $35 in interest for every $100 borrowed.
- **Number:** US$35 per US$100 per loan; 75% use the money to buy stock; 96% repay daily (2019 survey of 1,285 merchants)
- **Source:** Revista E&N (story by Diario El Mundo), "El Salvador: Mipymes pagan hasta 2,628 % de interés por usura", 9 Sep 2019, https://www.revistaeyn.com/finanzas/el-salvador-mipymes-pagan-hasta-2628-de-interes-por-usura-HAEN1317303
- **Exact quote:** "por cada préstamo de US$100 se cobran US$35 en interés"
- **Translation:** "for every US$100 loan, US$35 is charged in interest"
- **Use in:** one-pager / pitch / trifold / deck
- **Caveat:**
  - Survey by CIOPS (UTEC) of merchants in markets, small shops, tiendas and street vendors in five cities.
  - The data is from 2019, before the 2022 crackdown, so say the year.
  - The $35 is per loan. Borrowers renew up to 30 times a year, which is how annual rates run into the thousands (the survey's average: up to 2,628% a year).
  - The 96% say they pay daily for their physical safety.
  - 75%: "un 75 % destina este financiamiento para compra de mercadería para venta" ("75% use this money to buy stock to sell").
  - 96%: "un 96 % de los encuestados aseguró que debe pagar cada día por cuestiones de seguridad física" ("96% of those surveyed said they must pay every day for their physical safety"). Both copied from the page on 10 Nov 2026.
- **Checked:** Claude, 9 Nov 2026 (link opened, quote matches the page)

## 15. Credit bureaus
- **Claim:** El Salvador has three private credit bureaus, licensed and supervised by the SSF: Equifax, TransUnion and INFORED.
- **Number:** 3 bureaus: Equifax Centroamérica, S.A. de C.V.; TransUnion El Salvador, S.A. de C.V.; Información de Referencias Crediticias en Red, S.A. de C.V. (INFORED)
- **Source:**

  - Official list: an SSF letter naming the authorized bureaus (2014), https://transparencia.gob.sv/institutions/ssf/documents/75179/download
  - The law (SSF copy): https://www.ssf.gob.sv/descargas/Leyes/Leyes%20Financieras/Ley%20de%20Regulacion%20de%20los%20Servicios%20de%20Informacion%20sobre%20el%20Historial.pdf
- **Exact quote:** "las agencias autorizadas que operan en el país"
- **Translation:** "the authorized agencies that operate in the country"
- **Use in:** Q&A / deck

  - Bureaus only see formal credit, so a tienda's supplier credit and loan-shark debt never show up. Our repayment record fills that gap.
- **Checked:** Claude, 9 Nov 2026 (link opened, quote matches the page)

- **Claim:** The SSF runs a public credit registry, the Central de Riesgos, which lenders in the financial system can consult.
- **Number:** lenders can see the last 3 years of negative data
- **Source:**
  - Legislative Assembly, Decreto No. 128 (adds Art. 2-A to the credit-history law), approved 17 Aug 2021, Diario Oficial No. 166, 1 Sep 2021, https://transparencia.gob.sv/institutions/dc/documents/487833/download
  - Reporting rules: BCR, NRP-41, "Normas técnicas sobre el procedimiento para la recolección de información del Sistema Central de Riesgos" (in force 1 Jan 2024), https://transparencia.gob.sv/institutions/ssf/documents/582647/download
- **Exact quote:** "la Central de Riesgos de la Superintendencia del Sistema Financiero"
- **Translation:** "the Central Risk Registry of the Financial System Superintendency"
- **Use in:** Q&A / deck
- **Caveat:** Run by the SSF (the supervisor); the BCR (the regulator) writes the reporting rules. Only supervised lenders such as banks and cooperatives report to it, so informal credit is invisible here too. Both links are official copies on the government's transparency portal.
- **Checked:** Claude, 9 Nov 2026 (link opened, quote matches the page)

- **Number:** 9 digits; free to request; not required to operate
- **Source:** Dun & Bradstreet, "What is a D-U-N-S Number?", https://www.dnb.com/en-us/smb/duns/what-is-a-duns-number.html
- **Exact quote:** "a unique nine-digit identifier that links to your Dun & Bradstreet business credit file"
- **Translation:** n/a (English source)
- **Use in:** Q&A only
- **Caveat:**

## 18. The interest-rate cap law (Ley Contra la Usura)
- **Claim:** El Salvador caps interest rates by law. Each type of loan has a legal maximum, which the central bank calculates and publishes every six months.
- **Number:**
  - Cap: 1.6 times the average effective rate for each type of loan.
  - Published every January and July.
  - Fines: up to 500 minimum wages for unsupervised lenders, up to 1,000 for supervised ones.
- **Source:**
  - Legislative Assembly, Decreto No. 221, Ley Contra la Usura (Diario Oficial No. 16, 24 Jan 2013; in force 24 Feb 2013; reformed 2022), https://transparencia.gob.sv/institutions/dc/documents/578757/download
  - Explainer: El Diario de Hoy (elsalvador.com), "Las multas que podés enfrentar si prestás dinero en El Salvador fuera de los requisitos legales", 9 Jul 2026, https://www.elsalvador.com/dinero-y-negocios/finanzas-personales/prestamos-dinero-usara-multas-el-salvador/1282490/2026/
- **Exact quote:** "prohibir, prevenir y sancionar las prácticas usureras, protegiendo el patrimonio de las personas" (Art. 1, as quoted in the explainer)
- **Translation:** "to prohibit, prevent and punish usurious practices, protecting people's property"
- **Use in:** Q&A / deck (legal slide)
- **Caveat:**
  - Where each detail comes from:
    - 1.6× rule: the FLACSO opinion piece (Diario El Mundo, 21 Oct 2025, https://diario.elmundo.sv/opinion/la-ley-contra-la-usura-en-el-salvador-entre-la-proteccion-formal-y-la-exclusion-real-del-credito-mype) and a Consumer Protection Agency ruling (https://transparencia.gob.sv/institutions/dc/documents/476240/download).
    - January and July schedule: Consumer Protection Agency minutes (https://transparencia.gob.sv/institutions/dc/documents/265562/download).
    - Check Arts. 5 to 8 of the law for the exact article.

## 29. What we charge: the fee constants
- **Claim:** The distributor pays us 0.10% of an invoice paid by cash or digitally, or one 1.50% fee when the lender pool pays the invoice up front ("pay in 7 days"). The shop never pays a fee.
- **Number:**
  - Usage fee 0.10% (10 basis points) on cash and digital invoices. Per $100: the distributor receives $99.90 and we get $0.10.
  - Credit fee 1.50% (150 basis points), only on credit sales. Per $100: the distributor receives $98.50 at delivery. The $1.50 splits into $0.75 for the loss buffer, $0.50 for the lenders and $0.25 for us.
  - No usage fee on top of the credit fee.
- **Source:** `contracts/route_ledger/src/lib.rs` lines 17 to 29 (`USAGE_FEE_BPS`, `CREDIT_FEE_BPS`, `BUFFER_BPS`, `LENDER_BPS`, `OUR_BPS`) and the `pay` and `pay_with_credit` functions, commit dfdc33c. The app uses the same numbers (`lib/labels.ts:25`, `lib/mock.ts:18`). Also `docs/security.md` section 1 and `docs/context.md` section 5.
- **Exact quote:** "Credit sales don't pay it: our cut there is inside the 1.50% credit fee, so the distributor sees one number ($98.50 for every $100)." (code comment, line breaks removed)
- **Translation:** n/a (English source)
- **Use in:** one-pager / pitch / trifold / deck ("how do you make money")
- **Caveat:**
  - Demo parameters on testnet, set when the contract is deployed. Fees round down.
  - The shop pays the invoice's face value and no interest, but the distributor's 1.50% is a fee to be paid early. A lawyer should confirm it isn't treated as interest under the usury law (entry 18).
  - The cash usage fee is recorded (`get_usage_owed`) but nothing collects it yet (`docs/security.md` known limit 6). Digital payments collect it on the spot.
  - Don't say "$98.40 per $100" or "$0.35 per credit sale". Those add the 0.10% on top of the 1.50%, which the contract doesn't do. The draft assumptions doc (open PR #74) still shows them.
- **Checked:** Claude, 11 Nov 2026 (`lib.rs` read at dfdc33c; the split adds up: 98.50 + 0.25 + 0.75 + 0.50 = 100.00)

## 31. How a shop's credit limit grows and drops
- **Claim:** A shop's credit limit grows $25 each time it repays on time, up to $500. It drops $25 when it pays late, and a shop can't grow its limit by borrowing tiny amounts.
- **Number:**
  - Start: $200 in the demo (the admin sets it with `set_terms`).
  - +$25 (`LIMIT_STEP`) per on-time repayment, cap $500 (`LIMIT_CAP`).
  - It only grows if the shop borrowed at least $40 (`MIN_BORROW_TO_GROW`) in that cycle, and at most once per credit period (7 days; 2 minutes in the demo).
  - A late repayment drops it $25, never below $0. No new credit while a payment is overdue.
  - The limit changes only when the shop's whole balance is repaid.
- **Source:** `contracts/route_ledger/src/lib.rs` lines 31 to 39 and the `repay` function (line 574), commit dfdc33c; `scripts/deploy.sh` lines 113 and 115 (the $200); `docs/credit-model.md` sections 3 to 5.
- **Exact quote:** "Together these stop a shop from taking $1 of credit and repaying it over and over to reach $500." (code comment, line breaks removed)
- **Translation:** n/a (English source)
- **Use in:** pitch / deck / Q&A ("how do you stop fraud?")
- **Caveat:**
  - Demo parameters; they are guesses to test in interviews (#7), not real repayment data.
  - The contract has no default start limit; the $200 is set shop by shop at deploy. A limit the admin sets above $500 is left alone. `docs/credit-model.md` section 3 says the start should be the lower of $200 and one average delivery. That is a proposal, not built.
  - The limit is our own credit record. It is not a bureau score (entry 15).
- **Checked:** Claude, 11 Nov 2026 (`repay` and the constants read at dfdc33c)

## 32. When a shop never pays
- **Claim:** A shop four credit periods (28 days) past its due date can be written off. The loss buffer repays the lenders what it can, the shop's limit goes to $0, and the debt stays on its record.

- **Source:** `contracts/route_ledger/src/lib.rs` lines 41 to 43 and `cover_default` (line 637), commit dfdc33c; `scripts/deploy.sh` line 127; `docs/credit-model.md` sections 5 and 9.

- **Translation:** n/a (English source)
- **Use in:** Q&A / deck ("what if a shop doesn't pay?")
- **Caveat:**
  - The admin calls `cover_default` by hand. It is not automatic.
  - The buffer pays first. Anything past the buffer is a loss to the lenders (`docs/security.md` known limit 11), so never say "no risk" about the lenders. "No risk" is about the distributor, who is paid at delivery.
